# Vehicles and architecture

Check what each module is read as before polishing it. A crate on two legs can read as a head on a torso. A low load platform, visible fastening, distinct front sensor, and payload proportions can clarify the relationship; thicker legs alone cannot. For roofs, establish ridge, eaves, gable, and shared corners before tile marks. Mark directions must belong to that plane.

Retrieve `projection-scaffold`, `functional-machines`, or `constructive-architecture` with `reference_library.py techniques`. Map shared corners, axle or joint centers, and attachments in the construction plan. Build hidden scaffold edges when they resolve alignment, then remove them from the final visible grid. Mirror geometry before lighting, not a finished shaded module.

## Direction choices

- Express one function in the silhouette: speed, defense, cargo, power, worship, housing, industry, or landmark.
- Choose one projection and a scale cue: cockpit, door, person, wheel, tile, or window.
- Name structural modules before surface detail: hull/body, propulsion, control surface, support, roof, opening, foundation, or tower.
- Pick one useful signature that follows construction, not a decal on a generic body.

## Build

1. Arrange functional primitives and negative gaps. Map hidden edges mentally.
2. Make shared corners, vanishing steps, overlaps, and visible faces agree.
3. Add dark/mid/light planes from one source. Wrap stripes and edge highlights around volume.
4. Reuse window, panel, brick, vent, or light motifs with quiet gaps.
5. Weather after the structure reads. Keep distant or repeated modules lower contrast.

For isometric work, count a consistent 2:1 stair pattern. For interiors, settle wall/floor depth, occlusion, doorway scale, and furniture footprint before decoration.

## Reject

- mixed frontal, side, top-down, or isometric faces
- machine panels with no underlying hull or role
- buildings identified only by a sign
- stripes or highlights that ignore plane changes
- windows, bricks, or vents tiled at uniform density
- no scale cue where size is part of the brief
- detail used to disguise contradictory geometry
